<p>Every time you take control of a BNSF vehicle, the Vehicle Camera System needs to know who's behind the wheel. Associating yourself takes only a few seconds and keeps every trip, hours record, and safety event correctly attributed to you.</p>
    <h3>1. Log in at the start of your shift</h3>
    <p>When you start the vehicle for the first time that day, the camera will prompt: "Please log into the vehicle." Log in one of two ways:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>The easy way:</strong> open the Samsara Driver App, log in, select your vehicle, and drive.</li>
      <li><strong>The camera way:</strong> hold your driver's license or BNSF badge up to the camera so it can clearly read the ID number. Hold it in place until you hear the acceptance sound.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>2. If you don't log in right away</h3>
    <p>The system reminds you every 30 to 60 seconds until you log in. If you keep driving without logging in, a BNSF AI agent will call into your camera and ask you to log in using your B Number verbally.</p>
    <h3>3. Switching vehicles mid-shift</h3>
    <p>Taking control of a different vehicle? You must associate yourself again, even without a prompt. Turn on the vehicle, then either select the new vehicle in your Driver App, or hold your license/badge up to the camera and wait for the acceptance sound.</p>
    <h3>4. Logging out</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Idle or engine off for 45 minutes:</strong> you're logged out automatically, so the login prompt queues up for whoever takes the vehicle next.</li>
      <li><strong>End of shift:</strong> after 4 hours with no vehicle movement, the system logs you out automatically.</li>
      <li><strong>Logged in through the Driver App?</strong> Log yourself out there at the end of your shift too, to clear your association from the vehicle.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="callout tip"><strong>Why this matters</strong><p>Correct driver association keeps VCS footage, hours of service, and safety events tied to the right operator — protecting you and BNSF if a record ever needs to be reviewed.</p></div>
